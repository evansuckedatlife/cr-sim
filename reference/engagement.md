# Reach and tower support

`hits_to_kill.csv` is a community matrix of `ceil(hitpoints / damage)`. It is
useful and it is not enough, because two things it cannot express decide most
real fights. This is the computed answer to both, generated by
`cr_sim/data/engagement.py` from the current build.

Regenerate:

```bash
python -m cr_sim.cli interactions --write     # hit counts (interactions_generated.csv)
python -m cr_sim.cli engagement --write        # both files below
python -m pytest tests/test_engagement.py     # the reach/tower gate
```

Outputs, neither hand-edited:

| file | rows x cols | cell |
|---|---|---|
| `duels_generated.csv` | 5,253 pairs (4,908 decided) | winner, hits each way, seconds, free hits |
| `tower_assist_generated.csv` | 10,280 pairs | `hits alone / troop hits + tower hits` |

## What reach is worth

Ranges in this engine are hitbox-to-hitbox — `gap_between` subtracts both
collision radii before comparing against `attack_range` — so two units' ranges
are directly comparable, and the difference between them is a real distance
the shorter-ranged one walks while being shot.

Against a bare time-to-kill comparison, which starts both clocks together,
**reach flips the winner on 423 of 5,253 pairs (8.1%)**, and **52% of decided fights
are won without the winner being hit once**.

The engine agrees. Running the same pairs through real battles at two
starting distances, **starting distance alone flips the engine's own winner on
5.5% of pairs** — Knight beats Musketeer point blank and loses to her at
range; Mini P.E.K.K.A beats Wizard point blank and loses at range. That is not
a modelling artefact, it is the reason placement is a skill.

## What the Princess Tower is worth

Almost no defensive fight happens alone, so the hits *your* troop needs is not
`ceil(hitpoints / damage)` but whatever is left after the tower has taken its
share. The tower deals 92 per hit every 0.8s at tournament standard.

- It **changes the hit count on 92.3%** of (defender, attacker) pairs.
- Mean **4.8 hits saved**, max 68.
- On **591 pairs it finishes the job before the troop lands a single hit**.
- On **5,893 pairs it at least halves** what the troop has to do.

The cell carries the tower's own hits for a reason: `Fireball vs Musketeer` is
`2/1+1` — two casts alone, or one cast and a single tower hit, which is the
most-quoted interaction in the game. `Zap vs Witch` is also one cast, but
`1+8`: the tower does the work over the next six seconds. Reporting only the
troop column would flatten a finisher and a grind into the same number.

The extreme cases are the chip-damage cards, where nearly all the work was
never theirs: Firecracker needs 80 hits on a Golem alone and 12 with the tower
firing; Bats need 64 alone and 23. Reading either number off a 1v1 matrix
badly misprices the card.

## Checked against the engine

276 pairs from the 25-card sample, resolved both by arithmetic and by a real
`Battle`:

- **94.1% agree** at mutual-sight engagement range.
- **96.3% agree** at point blank.

All 16 residual disagreements are one of five named mechanics that no stat
profile can hold, not arithmetic errors:

| mechanic | pairs | why |
|---|---|---|
| Witch's spawned Skeletons | 8 | her profile carries only her own damage; the skeletons win the fight |
| Golem's death spawn | 2 | Golemites finish the swarm after he dies |
| Bowler knockback | 2 | the boulder pushes the target back, resetting the approach |
| Dark Prince charge | 2 | first-hit charge damage is not a flat `damage` field |
| splash / kamikaze timing | 2 | damage already in flight when someone dies |

## What the duel harness cannot check

`simulate_duel` strips the towers out, which means **a unit with nothing in
its sight range has nothing to walk toward and simply stands still**. Mini
P.E.K.K.A sees 5.5 tiles; a Musketeer shoots 6.0. Spawn them 6 tiles apart and
she never takes a step, never acquires a target, and is shot down stationary
over 403 ticks.

That is a fact about the harness, not about the cards — in a real match she is
walking at a tower the whole time. It manufactured a whole family of false
disagreements before it was spotted, so the cross-check now opens every fight
at `min(sight_a, sight_b)`, where both units can see each other. The
full-reach scenario is therefore *stated but not validated here*: no oracle in
this repo can currently test it, because testing it needs a tower to walk
toward.

Only two units in the build have a sight range shorter than their attack range
(`GoblinHut`, `RoyalDelivery`) and neither attacks, so sight never shortens a
real attacker's reach.

## What the model still assumes

Head-on, one against one, the shorter-ranged unit walking straight in. No
pathing around a third body, no retargeting, no splash catching a second unit,
no rage or slow, and the tower already has its target in range at tick zero.
Those assumptions are what make it arithmetic instead of a simulation, and the
five mechanics in the table above are what they cost.
